# Learning from Trajectories: How Agent Systems Turn Experience into Knowledge — a Literature Review for Kapso

**Scope.** This review synthesizes 22 paper cards (covering ~24 papers, since the Generative Agents card also covers MemoryBank and the survey card also covers its companion empirical study) on how LLM-agent systems convert raw execution trajectories into reusable knowledge. It is written for the design of kapso's "learn from trajectories" feature: a mechanism that consumes evolve-campaign trajectories plus the current knowledge bank and updates the bank with mixed text/code cards carrying reliability scores. Every empirical number below is attributed to its paper; where the cards are thin or conflict, that is stated explicitly.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**The landscape in brief.** The field has moved through three recognizable generations, which the survey (arXiv 2605.06716) formalizes as Storage → Reflection → Experience. Generation one (Reflexion, Generative Agents, 2023) established that LLM-written verbal abstractions, stored and re-injected, shape later behavior — Reflexion abstracting task *outcomes* (its ablation shows abstracted lessons add +8% over storing raw failed trajectories on HotPotQA, 80% vs 72%, beating raw retry), Generative Agents abstracting *observations* with no reward signal at all (importance is an a-priori LLM poignancy guess, not an outcome measure, and evaluation is believability, not task success). Generation two (ExpeL, Voyager, CLIN, AutoGuide, AWM, DS-Agent, 2023–2024) made the memory persistent and cross-task, splitting into a text-insight lineage (ExpeL's insight pool with ADD/UPVOTE/DOWNVOTE/EDIT operations, AutoGuide's state-conditioned guidelines) and a code-skill lineage (Voyager's verify-before-store executable library, AWM's induced workflows). Generation three (2025–2026: ACE, Dynamic Cheatsheet, Memento, MemRL, RoMeRL, ASI, SkillRevise, A-MEM, TF-GRPO) confronts the lifecycle and merge problems the earlier work punted on: outcome-trained utility scores (MemRL's Q-values, Memento's BCE-trained case utilities), deterministic delta-merging to avoid LLM rewrite collapse (ACE), insert-time neighbor evolution (A-MEM), and — critically — the first formal failure-mode analysis of scored memory (RoMeRL's memory-reward trap and cold-Q staleness theorems).

A caveat that applies to the entire corpus: **every system reviewed operates on minutes-scale, cheap-feedback episodes** (household text games, web navigation, single-shot QA, one-script Kaggle tasks). None operates in kapso's regime of multi-hour, dollar-cost campaigns yielding a handful of noisy scalar outcome signals. The taxonomy and mechanisms transfer; the statistical regimes behind the headline numbers mostly do not, and this asymmetry drives several of the design conclusions below.

**The design conclusions the literature most strongly supports for kapso:**

1. **Abstract, don't archive.** Storing raw trajectories without distillation buys little or actively hurts: Reflexion's no-self-reflection ablation gained 0% on hard coding tasks; Dynamic Cheatsheet's full-history condition scored *below* the no-memory baseline (GPT-4o AIME: 13.3% vs 20%); the survey's companion study found raw-trajectory reuse produced −9.5% forward transfer on ALFWorld where abstracted insights gave +6.5%. Kapso should never inject raw transcripts or full changes.log into ideation.
2. **Contrast is the strongest abstraction signal.** The best extraction mechanisms are contrastive: success/failure pairs on the same task (ExpeL, AutoGuide), within-group winner/loser comparison (Training-Free GRPO, whose ablation shows group size 1 loses most of the gain). Kapso's K parallel lanes per iteration are a natural "group," and features_history.md's TESTED-KEPT/TESTED-REJECTED entries are ready-made contrastive pairs with measured deltas.
3. **Verify before storing, especially for code.** Voyager's ablation makes its self-verification gate the single most valuable component (~73% fewer discoveries without it); ASI's execution-based replay gate admits only ~15.6% of candidate skills and its verified variants beat unverified by up to 7.5 points; SkillRevise shows one-shot LLM-authored skills are only marginally better than no skill in aggregate (39.53% vs 36.05%, +3.5pp) and per-task sometimes *worse than no skill*, until trace-conditioned revision lifts them to 61.63%. Kapso's immutable grader is precisely the trustworthy admission signal most of these systems lacked.
4. **Score on attributable outcomes, never on co-occurrence or retrieval frequency.** RoMeRL proves formally and empirically that episode-level reward shared across all co-retrieved memories inflates useless items (the memory-reward trap) and that growing banks go stale (~45% of items never re-scored). Generative Agents and MemoryBank reinforce on *access frequency* — a popularity signal, not a correctness signal. Kapso's reliability score must update only from experiments that demonstrably exercised the card.
5. **Never let an LLM rewrite the bank wholesale.** ACE's motivating failure is "context collapse": a monolithic LLM rewrite compressed an 18,282-token memory to 122 tokens and dropped accuracy 66.7%→57.1%. Dynamic Cheatsheet documents the same pathology (models abbreviate instead of faithfully re-emitting). Merging must be itemized: LLM proposes deltas; deterministic code applies them to cards with stable identity.
6. **Mixed text+code representation is empirically justified, with routing rules.** Dynamic Cheatsheet's single strongest result (Game of 24: 10%→99%) came from a stored code artifact; AWM found text ≈ code for prompt-injected *guidance* but executable macros failed when steps needed runtime adaptation; ASI found verified programs injected as callable *actions* beat the same content as prompt text (+3.7 points). Deterministic, verifiable, adaptation-free procedures → code cards injected at the execution site; contextual, evidential knowledge → text cards injected at ideation.
7. **Cards need explicit applicability conditions.** AutoGuide's decomposition of knowledge into (context key, guideline) beats undifferentiated tips (WebShop 46% vs ExpeL's 35%); AutoManual mandates scope-first phrasing; Mobile-Agent-E attaches preconditions to shortcuts (and documents that LLM-side precondition checking still fails, arguing for machine-checkable conditions). Injection budgets are small: AutoGuide's k=5 hurts vs k=2–3; Memento's K>8 hurts; DS-Agent found k=1 best for code exemplars. And the budget's floor is zero: ACE found no benefit on tasks wanting concise instructions (HotPotQA) or a single rule (Game of 24), where extra context is redundant — for some task types the correct playbook contribution is *nothing*.
8. **Post-storage lifecycle is the field's biggest hole — and kapso's planned reliability score fills a real, documented gap.** ExpeL freezes counts after extraction; Voyager, AWM, ASI, DS-Agent, AutoGuide, Learn-by-interact, A-MEM, and Mobile-Agent-E all admit units once and never demote them, and several document the resulting contamination (AWM: incorrect workflows from judge false-positives "degrade model performance" permanently; Mobile-Agent-E: erroneous shortcuts persist and keep injecting errors).

**Genuinely open problems** (expanded in §8): scoring under extremely sparse outcome signals (tens of campaign outcomes, not hundreds of episodes); credit assignment from a multi-hour campaign to individual cards; contradiction resolution between cards (essentially unsolved in all 22 papers); merging *quantitative* evidence (measured deltas) rather than propositions; and choosing abstraction level with too few samples for DreamCoder-style compression statistics.

## 2. A Taxonomy of the Field

The most informative axis is the **reusable unit of experience**. Five families, with several systems deliberately hybrid:

**A. Episodic cases (raw or lightly-summarized exemplars).** The unit stays close to the trajectory; generalization is pushed into retrieval/scoring. Memento (whole-task (state, plan, reward) triplets with learned Q-retrieval), MemRL (intent/experience/Q triplets), RoMeRL (four bounded representative-trajectory slots per task group), Learn-by-interact (relabeled sub-trajectory demonstrations), DS-Agent's agent bank (verbatim (task, code) pairs), Reflexion's episodic buffer (within-task only).

**B. Text insights and rules.** LLM-distilled natural-language knowledge. ExpeL (insight pool with vote counts), AutoGuide (state-conditioned guidelines), CLIN (typed causal sentences with linguistic confidence), TF-GRPO (experiential library from group contrasts), ACE (itemized bullets with helpful/harmful counters), Generative Agents (cited reflection trees) + MemoryBank (summarization ladder with Ebbinghaus decay), A-MEM (annotated, linked notes).

**C. Workflows and procedural documents.** Multi-step routines in structured text. AWM (variable-abstracted sub-task workflows), SkillRevise (task-level skill documents plus a principle memory of repair patterns), AutoManual (typed rules compiled into a manual — straddles B and C).

**D. Executable code skills.** Voyager (verified JavaScript skills), ASI (verified Python functions injected into the action space), DreamCoder (λ-calculus library primitives admitted by corpus compression).

**E. Explicit hybrids.** Mobile-Agent-E (Tips = text; Shortcuts = executable JSON macros with preconditions), Dynamic Cheatsheet (one curated text blob containing both strategies and inline code), DS-Agent (dual banks: human-insight text + agent code), AutoManual (rules + skill/reflection libraries).

The survey (2605.06716) is meta-level and confirms this trichotomy of "explicit experience" (NL rules / executable code / skill libraries with lifecycle), adding implicit (weights) and hybrid (cache-then-compress-into-weights) routes that are out of scope for kapso's bank.

## 3. Q1 — Abstraction from Trajectories

### 3.1 The mechanism menu

**(a) Single-trajectory reflection** (Reflexion, CLIN, MemRL, Mobile-Agent-E). One LLM call converts {trajectory, outcome} into a lesson. Cheapest, weakest signal: it depends entirely on the model's self-credit-assignment. Evidence that even this beats nothing: Reflexion +22% absolute on AlfWorld over ReAct retry; and that the *abstraction step itself* carries value: Reflexion's episodic-memory-only variant (raw failed trajectory stored, no verbal reflection) gets 72% on HotPotQA vs 80% for full Reflexion. But TF-GRPO's ablation shows "directly generated experiences" (one-shot reflection without an outcome-grounded loop) failed to improve performance at all — isolated reflection unanchored to comparative evidence is unreliable.

**(b) Contrastive extraction** (ExpeL, AutoGuide, TF-GRPO, and the survey's "contrastive induction" family). Knowledge is defined as the *delta* between a success and a failure sharing a prefix. ExpeL feeds fail/success pairs on the same task plus batches of L successes; AutoGuide sharpens this by locating the deviating timestep and splitting the learned unit into WHEN (context abstracted from the shared prefix) and WHAT (guideline from the divergent continuation) — its WebShop ablation is the cleanest granularity evidence in the corpus: ReAct 30% → context-identification alone 36% → guidelines without context-conditioning 37% → full state-conditioned guidelines 46%. TF-GRPO generalizes contrast to groups: G rollouts per query, lessons extracted only when the group contains both winners and losers, with the group-size-1 ablation (82.7/73.3 → 80.4/69.3 on AIME24/25) showing the contrast, not the reflection, drives the gain.

**(c) Backward construction / post-hoc relabeling** (Learn-by-interact). Instead of trusting the original goal, enumerate nested sub-trajectories and have an LLM write a fresh instruction describing what each *actually accomplished* — salvaging drifted and failed rollouts. Removing this relabeling collapses ICL gains to ~2% and costs up to 14.0% in the training setting. Notable granularity finding: short (<5-step) sub-trajectory units were most effective, mixed lengths best overall.

**(d) Workflow/skill induction** (AWM, ASI, AutoManual). Extract recurring *sub-task* routines from successful trajectories, with variables abstracted ({product-name}). AWM's argument for sub-task granularity: sub-routines recur across tasks where full-task exemplars (Synapse-style) do not — and its online induction recovers cross-website/domain generalization (33.9/35.5 step SR vs MindAct's 18.6) that offline full-trajectory approaches lose. ASI adds explicit granularity control by prompt ("mediocre complexity," 3–10 lines) plus episode cleaning before induction (87.9 → 13.4 tokens/step).

**(e) Program synthesis + compression** (DreamCoder; Stitch/LILO/TroVE as leads). The only *principled* answer to "what deserves to be an abstraction": a fragment is promoted exactly when it compresses the description length of (library + all solved programs), with the generality of the fragment (which subterms become parameters) chosen by the same objective. Over-specific fragments (used once) and over-general ones (compress nothing) both lose automatically. Library depth correlates with solve rate at r=0.79 (DreamCoder). The cost: it requires many verified solutions per cycle and crisp symbolic artifacts — the authors themselves restrict scope to noise-free domains.

**(f) Importance-gated batch reflection** (Generative Agents). Don't reflect per event; accumulate importance and reflect when a threshold (150) trips, using LLM-generated "salient questions" as the extraction scaffold, storing insights with citations to evidence ("because of 1, 5, 3") — the corpus's cleanest provenance mechanism, forming multi-level trees where reflections cite reflections.

**(g) Deliberate non-abstraction** (Memento, RoMeRL). Store episodes raw; push generalization into learned retrieval (Memento's Q) or bounded representative slots (RoMeRL). Honest and effective in high-repetition regimes, but Memento concedes whole-task granularity limits transfer to tasks resembling whole old tasks, and MemRL's plateau on HLE (mean intra-task similarity 0.186) shows episodic similarity-keyed memory fails precisely when tasks are heterogeneous — kapso's regime across RelBench task types.

### 3.2 Granularity: what the evidence says

Three convergent findings. First, **fine-grained beats monolithic for transfer, but costs stability**: the survey's companion study found per-insight entries (Cond-Ind) lift BabyAI forward transfer from +9.0 to +15.0 vs bundled entries, but with the most severe forgetting (BWT −10.0); ACE argues one-insight-per-bullet explicitly to enable localized updates and avoid the "brevity bias" of monolithic rewrites. Second, **the right level is decision-scale, not campaign-scale**: AutoGuide's guidelines are single decision points; AWM's workflows are sub-tasks; Learn-by-interact's best units are <5 steps. Whole-campaign postmortems stored verbatim are Reflection-stage artifacts in the survey's terms — context-bound and brittle to "minor shifts in context." Third, **generality should be earned, not asserted**: CLIN documents transferred insights silently over-generalizing at first use in a new environment (the stove/oven failure); SkillRevise's defect taxonomy quantifies authored-skill failures as over_specificity (54/208) *and* wrong_abstraction_level (54/208) alongside near-universal context_pollution (207/208); DreamCoder is the existence proof that generality can be selected by an objective rather than a prompt. A practical middle path the cards support: CLIN's two-tier scheme (instance-bound cards first; meta-cards produced by a second de-binding pass over several campaigns, marked unverified-in-new-context until confirmed) and the survey's MDL sanity test (if the card is nearly as long as the evidence it summarizes, it has not abstracted). CLIN also supplies the corpus's only cross-environment transfer numbers for two-tier abstraction, and they back the scheme: meta-memory gives +4.1 zero-shot on unseen environment variants, rising to +20.9 once adaptation trials continue in the new environment, and +13 zero-shot on transfer to related tasks — de-bound cards carry real signal, but most of their value is unlocked only after in-context re-verification.

### 3.3 How failure trajectories are used — a real split in the field

- **Failures ignored entirely**: Voyager, ASI, AWM, DreamCoder, DS-Agent (success-only retention; DS-Agent uses failures only transiently for re-ranking). This is a survivorship-bias channel the code-skill lineage never closed.
- **Failures only in contrast with successes**: ExpeL (pairs require an eventual success), AutoGuide, TF-GRPO (uniform-failure groups teach nothing — a real problem early in hard campaigns).
- **Failures as first-class stored units**: Memento and MemRL store failure cases with equal standing and let outcomes adjudicate — MemRL found ~12% of failure-derived memories earned top-bin Q, direct evidence against pre-filtering to successes. RoMeRL reserves two of its four slots for failures (freshest failure + proven-informative failure, and ablating the latter hurts). AutoManual mints "Corrected Error" and "Unsolved Error" rule types; Mobile-Agent-E feeds error history into tip extraction; SkillRevise is *driven* by failure diagnosis.

For kapso the third stance is clearly right: technical_difficulties records and TESTED-REJECTED ledger entries are precisely the negative evidence the success-only systems discard, and MemRL/RoMeRL show scored lifecycles can sort useful failures from noise. One further gate matters before any failure writes to the bank: AutoManual's Imperfect-Rules vs Imperfect-Agent triage — an experiment that failed because the implementation agent botched execution must generate *no* knowledge update (and must not decrement the card it was testing).

## 4. Q2 — Text vs Code Representation

### 4.1 Who uses which

Text-only: Reflexion, ExpeL, AutoGuide, CLIN, TF-GRPO, ACE, MemRL, A-MEM, Generative Agents/MemoryBank. Code-only: Voyager, ASI, DreamCoder. Deliberately mixed: Mobile-Agent-E (Tips + Shortcuts — the cleanest structural analogue of kapso's text/code cards, though its card carries a caveat the analogy must inherit: no ablation separates Tips-only from Shortcuts-only, so its evolution gain supports learning-as-a-whole, not the two-representation split specifically), Dynamic Cheatsheet (code inline in a text sheet), DS-Agent (text insight bank + code case bank), AutoManual (text rules + code skill library). AWM sits in between: workflow content in either surface form.

### 4.2 The key experimental results

- **Code can carry near-total task performance when the procedure truly generalizes**: Dynamic Cheatsheet's Game of 24 result (GPT-4o 10% → 99%) is one stored Python brute-force solver reused ~100 times; its knowledge-domain gains (GPQA +9.1, MMLU-Pro Physics +8) came from text summaries — an in-one-paper demonstration of the routing principle.
- **Surface form matters less than content for prompt-injected guidance**: AWM found text vs code formatting of the same workflow negligible (45.4 vs 45.1 step SR) when the agent *imitates* the workflow.
- **But representation-at-use-site matters when code is executable**: ASI's ablation ladder — unverified text 32.6% < verified program-in-memory 36.4% < verified text-in-memory 39.0% < verified program-as-callable-action 40.1% — separates the verification effect from the injection-site effect (+3.7 for action-space over prompt text with identical content).
- **Executable macros fail when steps must adapt at runtime**: AWM's AWM-AS variant (workflows as callable actions) gained only +1.3 and was invoked just 18.5% of the time — static action sequences break on dynamic state (pop-up selectors), which is why flexible text guidance won there while ASI's programs (which contain conditionals/control flow) won in their setting.
- **Generic text helps little where precise procedural behavior is required**: ExpeL's WebShop gain is marginal (mean reward 0.701 vs 0.665, conceded to be only near the low end of Reflexion's range), and its card names the cause — generic textual insights help little on tasks needing precise procedural behavior. An independent datum, from inside the text-only lineage itself, for routing procedural knowledge to code cards.
- **Self-authored skills are unreliable until exercised**: the 2026 evidence is unambiguous. SkillRevise: one-shot Skill-Creator skills score 39.53% vs 36.05% no-skill on SkillsBench — sometimes *below* no-skill per task — and only trace-conditioned revision reaches 61.63%; the ablation puts −29pp on removing execution-trace diagnosis. Mobile-Agent-E documents both failure directions for unvalidated code units: shortcut *misuse* (LLM precondition checks fail against misperceived state) and *erroneous self-generated shortcuts* (missing steps) that persist with no revision path. Voyager's admission critic occasionally misses success even with environment grounding.
- **Different consumers want different representations**: DS-Agent found homogeneous (task, code) cases beat textual insights for one-pass code generation at deployment, while textual insights powered exploratory planning during development; Mobile-Agent-E injects Shortcuts into the *planner* (they change what gets planned) and Tips into the *operator*.

### 4.3 The decision rule the papers collectively support

A learning becomes a **CODE card** when all of the following hold:

1. **Deterministic and self-contained** — a fixed procedure whose steps do not require in-context judgment (AWM-AS failure; ASI's permitted-primitives + control-flow constraint).
2. **Executably verifiable** — it can be replayed against a real evaluation and attested by an immutable signal (Voyager's environment-attested gate; ASI's three-condition replay: correctness + actually-invoked + observable effect; kapso analogue: re-run against an archived run and match/beat the recorded grader score).
3. **Recurring / compressive** — it would shorten more than one past solution if factored out (DreamCoder's admission objective; the survey's suggestion that code cards require recurrence across ≥2 campaigns).
4. **Equipped with checkable preconditions** — machine-checkable applicability conditions (dataset schema, task type, environment), because LLM-judged preconditions demonstrably fail (Mobile-Agent-E).

Everything else — empirical findings with effect sizes, negative results, causal hypotheses, environment facts, hedged heuristics — stays a **TEXT card**, because text (a) carries uncertainty and scope gracefully (CLIN's SHOULD/MAY hedging, printed into the card so the reading LLM can weigh it), (b) can be adapted rather than executed, and (c) can cite evidence (Generative Agents' citation trees). Injection routing follows the DS-Agent/Mobile-Agent-E split: code cards → implementation agents (ideally as callable tools/scripts per ASI, not pasted text), text cards → ideation. A card can migrate: a text card describing a procedure that later proves deterministic and recurrent is a code-card candidate — but only through the verification gate, never by authoring alone (SkillRevise's 39.5%-vs-61.6% gap is the standing warning).

## 5. Q3 — Scoring, Validation, and Lifecycle

### 5.1 The full menu found in the corpus

**Validation before storage (admission gates):**
- *Execution-attested success*: Voyager (self-verification critic + environment state check; most valuable component by ablation), ASI (trajectory replay with skill substituted; ~15.6% pass rate — a realistic prior for code-card yield), DreamCoder (units derive only from verified solutions *and* must improve the global objective).
- *Measured-improvement retention*: DS-Agent (archive a (task, code) case only when held-out performance improved) — with the evidential caveat that the retain step's marginal value (agent bank vs seed-only bank) is only indirectly ablated, so the gate is a sensible template rather than a cleanly proven one.
- *LLM-committee plausibility*: Learn-by-interact (all-members-must-agree filter, 25–50% pass) — explicitly *not* outcome verification; a plausible-sounding wrong procedure passes.
- *Revision-before-banking*: SkillRevise (at least one execute→diagnose→revise cycle before a skill is trusted; early rounds dominate the budget sweep, 54.65% at B=1 → 65.12% at B=5).
- *Online practice*: AutoManual (rules are revised in the loop that uses them; its offline-extraction ablation, 97.4% → 77.6%, is the largest single-factor result in the corpus for validate-by-practice).
- *Judged-success admission*: AWM (workflows induced only from success-labeled trajectories — ground truth offline, an LLM judge online). The cautionary member of the menu: the judge's error rate is never measured, and its false-positive rollouts contaminate memory permanently (§5.2(4)) — a judged gate without a quantified judge is admission theater.
- *None*: Reflexion, ExpeL, AutoGuide, CLIN, A-MEM, Mobile-Agent-E, Memento, MemRL, Dynamic Cheatsheet.

**Post-storage scoring:**
- *Extraction-time vote counters, then frozen*: ExpeL (ADD=2, UPVOTE/EDIT +1, DOWNVOTE −1, remove at 0 — the counter encodes extractor re-endorsement over training batches, not downstream outcomes). The count is not only frozen, it is never *consumed*: the paper specifies no use of it at inference — all surviving insights are concatenated into the prompt regardless of count — so kapso's score-as-retrieval-weight is a second departure, beyond merely unfreezing the counter.
- *Use-time attribution counters*: ACE (helpful/harmful counters incremented when the Generator flags a bullet as useful/misleading during a real attempt — the closest existing thing to kapso's confirm/contradict design, though retirement is never counter-driven).
- *Outcome-trained value estimates*: MemRL (per-memory EMA, Q ← Q + α(r−Q), updated only for injected memories; Q predicts success 21.5%→88.1% across bins, Pearson r=0.861), Memento (state-*conditional* utility p(success | state, case) trained with BCE — a case is good *for similar states*, not globally; monotone gains over 5 continual-learning iterations), RoMeRL (EMA over exactly 4 utility variables per task group, with warm-start inheritance on replacement).
- *Group-relative implicit validation*: TF-GRPO (no per-unit score; an unhelpful experience shows up as continued losers in later groups and gets deleted/modified — validation-by-re-rollout).
- *Retrieval-time composite scores*: Generative Agents (recency × importance × relevance, equal weights, min-max normalized; recency decays from last *access*), MemRL/RoMeRL (rank = (1−λ)·similarity + λ·utility, both z-score normalized, λ≈0.5 optimal; removing normalization measurably raises forgetting, 0.041→0.073 in MemRL).
- *Decay*: MemoryBank (Ebbinghaus R = e^(−t/S), S incremented per recall — frequently recalled memories approach permanence, unrecalled ones fade probabilistically).
- *Compression-prior weighting*: DreamCoder (frequently reused primitives get high prior mass; useless ones sink without deletion — soft retirement via ranking, the same pattern as Memento's never-wins-top-K).
- *Linguistic confidence*: CLIN (SHOULD vs MAY encoded in the unit's text, updated by LLM judgment — the primitive ancestor of a numeric score; its ablation shows the structured-confidence representation beats free-form advice by 6.2 points).
- *Multi-term utility*: SkillRevise's selection utility U = α·Δ_succ + β·g_succ·Δ_eff + γ·Δ_trans − λ·C_interf — the only formula in the corpus with explicit efficiency, transfer, and *interference* terms.
- *Usage-based trimming*: not in the 22 core papers but in their leads (TroVE prunes functions unused by recent solutions) — the retirement mechanism the code-library papers lack; AWM's post-hoc utility rate (0.91–0.94 of stored workflows actually exercised) is the measurement without the mechanism.

### 5.2 What goes wrong — the documented failure modes

1. **The memory-reward trap (reward misattribution)** — RoMeRL, the corpus's only formal treatment: when episode-level reward is shared uniformly across co-retrieved memories, an item with zero or negative marginal contribution can accumulate positive utility (its Theorem 1 shows attribution bias is *irreducible* by more outcome-level feedback), and exploration bonuses amplify it (MemRL+UCB let injected noise items collect 3× the positive-update mass RoMeRL did in the stress test, 7.2 vs 2.4). MemRL itself acknowledges the uniform-credit problem and defers Shapley attribution to future work.
2. **Score staleness / cold scores** — RoMeRL: estimating N utilities to error ε needs O(N log N/ε²) feedback; empirically ~45% of items in a growing bank had *never* been updated since admission ("cold-Q"), cut to 9% only by bounding the number of scored variables to 4 per task group. ExpeL's freeze-after-extraction and Generative Agents' never-re-rated importance scores are the same pathology by construction.
3. **Popularity ≠ correctness** — Generative Agents' access-refreshed recency and MemoryBank's recall-incremented S both reinforce on retrieval frequency, creating a rich-get-richer loop with no connection to whether the memory was ever *right*. RoMeRL notes the same entrenchment risk in utility-weighted retrieval (high-score items get retrieved more, hence updated more).
4. **Corrupted evaluators poison the signal** — Reflexion's MBPP result is the canonical case: self-generated flaky unit tests (16.3% false-positive rate) made the agent stop on wrong solutions and *lose* to the plain GPT-4 baseline (77.1% vs 80.1%). AWM's online pipeline inherits its LLM judge's unquantified error rate and documents permanent contamination from false-positive rollouts; ASI's verification shares a failure mode with its LM evaluator. ACE reports that without ground truth or execution signals, noisy Reflector insights pollute the context and performance degrades.
5. **Context collapse from LLM-mediated maintenance** — ACE (18,282→122 tokens, 66.7%→57.1%) and Dynamic Cheatsheet (curators abbreviate — "Previous content [...] preserved" — silently corrupting the sheet). CLIN's full-rewrite-per-trial has the same shape: no persistence guarantee, no audit trail for why a unit vanished.
6. **Unvalidated units persist forever** — AWM (incorrect workflows admitted once degrade performance with no recovery path), Mobile-Agent-E (erroneous shortcuts keep injecting errors; the authors name revision-of-shortcuts as their top future work), ASI (deprecated skills linger; safety rests on the agent not calling them), AutoGuide (a guideline extracted from n=1 contrastive pair is trusted permanently).
7. **Reward-hacking the memory** — essentially undiscussed: ACE explicitly has no defense against the Generator gaming its own helpful/harmful flags, and no paper in the corpus stress-tests an agent optimizing its memory's scores rather than the task. The nearest tool is RoMeRL's MRT stress protocol (inject known-noise items, measure their positive-update mass), which is reusable for auditing any scored-memory design.
8. **Memory adherence ≠ memory quality (over-adherence and rule-ignoring)** — AWM documents agents following workflows even when not relevant to the current state, with difficulty diverging from them: action F1 slightly *drops* even as element accuracy rises. AutoManual documents the mirror image — the Planner sometimes ignores rules or hallucinates observations, which is exactly why its Imperfect-Agent triage branch exists. A good bank can still be used badly in both directions. Kapso implication: injected cards can anchor and homogenize the ideation ensemble — compounding the companion study's 88.5% identical-retrieval collapse (§9) — so card influence on ideation diversity should be monitored, not assumed benign.
9. **Reflection loops hit local minima that more memory cannot escape** — Reflexion on WebShop showed no improvement after 4 trials: reflections keep restating the same diagnosis, and verbal reflection cannot rescue tasks needing genuinely creative exploration. (Its cleanest companion datum: zero tasks failed on trial 1 by any no-memory baseline were ever solved in later trials — retry without memory is useless, but retry *with* memory has a ceiling.) This bounds what text-card learning can fix: a kapso campaign stuck in an idea basin will not be rescued by more lessons; it needs exploration pressure, which is the evolve loop's job, not the bank's.

### 5.3 The most defensible composite lifecycle for kapso cards

One tension should be named before composing, because the menu above is really the field's two alternative bets: **hard admission gate, then no lifecycle** (Voyager, ASI, DreamCoder, DS-Agent — trust the gate, never revisit) versus **soft or no admission, then an outcome-driven lifecycle** (ACE, MemRL, Memento — admit cheaply, let scores correct). No paper stacks both, and stacking has yield arithmetic: ASI's ~15.6% replay pass rate applied to kapso's handful of code-card candidates per campaign predicts on the order of *one admitted code card every few campaigns*. The composite below therefore splits the bets deliberately by representation: text cards take the soft-gate-plus-lifecycle bet (admission on cited evidence, correction by score — cheap admission, recoverable errors), while the hard replay gate is reserved for code cards, whose failure mode (a silently wrong executable) is the costlier one and whose near-zero throughput is acceptable.

Synthesizing the menu against the failure modes, the literature best supports a five-part lifecycle. (Parts 1–4 each have direct precedent; part 5 is where kapso extends the field.)

1. **Admission**: text cards require cited, measured evidence (a features_history delta, a root-caused technical_difficulties entry) — never judge prose alone (Reflexion's MBPP lesson: reliability updates only from officially graded runs). Code cards additionally require ASI-style execution verification against an archived run, and per SkillRevise should not initialize above a low prior without at least one revision cycle against execution evidence.
2. **A numeric score updated only on attributable outcomes**: MemRL's EMA rule Q ← Q + α(r−Q) is the simplest defensible update (it has a convergence guarantee), but gated by attribution — update only cards the experiment demonstrably exercised (cited in the spec and named load-bearing by the judge/features_history), not all cards injected into a winning campaign (RoMeRL's trap). AutoManual's Imperfect-Agent gate applies: execution-botched experiments update nothing. Whether the score should be global (MemRL) or state-conditional (Memento's p(success | context, card) via kernel episodic control) is genuinely open; Memento's version is more expressive but needs more signal than kapso may have. Two parameters the rule needs that the corpus does supply: **initialization** — a new card's score is seeded from its measured evidence, not a flat default: delta magnitude and replication count set the prior (Learn-by-interact's relabel carries the measured delta as the card's initial evidence; CLIN sets confidence by delta magnitude and replication count), code cards inherit the real validation score of the run that minted them (Voyager) but stay capped at a low prior until a revision cycle against execution evidence (SkillRevise, per part 1); and the **r-mapping** — r=1 for a TESTED-KEPT entry whose delta clears the §7.3(1) significance threshold, r=0/−1 for TESTED-REJECTED or a grader-contradicted delta (the MemRL card's mapping), with sub-threshold deltas updating nothing.
3. **A visit counter alongside the score**: RoMeRL's cold-Q finding makes "low score, well-tested" (retire) and "low score, barely tested" (keep exploring) different lifecycle states; with kapso's tens-of-outcomes regime, most cards will be cold most of the time, so the counter is not optional bookkeeping — it is the main guard against trusting stale defaults. The action rules the states imply, with their trigger values flagged as open tuning choices exactly as §7.3(1) flags the significance threshold: a cold card (no exercise within N campaigns) is *down-ranked* in retrieval, never excluded — it must retain a path back to being exercised; retirement fires only on the conjunction low score AND visits above a minimum (the "well-tested" condition); neither N, the retire threshold, nor the visit minimum has corpus guidance in kapso's regime.
4. **Retrieval ranking as soft retirement, decay as hard retirement**: rank cards into ideation by (1−λ)·similarity + λ·normalized-reliability (MemRL/RoMeRL, λ≈0.5, z-score both terms), so low scorers sink out of prompts long before formal action (DreamCoder's prior-weighting pattern); add a MemoryBank-style curve with S incremented on *confirmation* rather than recall — cards never re-confirmed decay toward archival, repeatedly-validated cards approach permanence, and contradictions decrement or reset S (the extension both memory papers lack). Retire to archive with citations intact, never delete (Generative Agents shows unpruned memory measurably degrades decisions; provenance must survive for audit). When a card *supersedes* another — an edit or a merge-time replacement winning over the incumbent — adopt RoMeRL's warm-start utility inheritance: the successor inherits the incumbent's score with its visit counter reset, carrying RoMeRL's own caveat that a bad successor rides the old card's credibility until enough updates correct it. The decay curve's parameters (S increments, archival threshold) likewise have no corpus guidance and are open tuning choices.
5. **Extensions with weaker precedent, flagged as design choices**: SkillRevise's interference term (a card that helps its home task family but hurts others gets penalized — measurable across kapso's parallel branches) and a no-card fallback arm (SkillRevise: a task solvable without a skill failed after revision; a card must be able to lose to nothing — independently supported by ACE's non-universality result, §1(7): on some task types the correct playbook contribution is zero cards). Neither has been run inside a scored lifecycle anywhere in the corpus.

## 6. Q4 — Merging into an Existing Bank

### 6.1 The mechanisms that exist

**Operation vocabularies (edit-vs-add made structural).** ExpeL's extractor must emit ADD/EDIT/UPVOTE/DOWNVOTE against the current insight set held in context — a duplicate *becomes* an upvote, disagreement becomes a downvote; the operation set itself forces the update-vs-create decision. TF-GRPO adds an explicit merge op ("merge similar experiences into a more general form") and shows the bank must be in-context during extraction so genuinely-new lessons are proposed rather than duplicates. AutoManual makes it tool calls (write_rule vs update_rule(rule_id)), and Mem0 (a lead, cited by Memento and MemRL) productionizes ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP. Caveat: in all of these, merge quality rests on LLM instruction-following — ExpeL documents extraction dropping 39%→32% with a weaker extractor.

**Deterministic delta merging (the anti-collapse design).** ACE's Curator: the LLM only proposes small delta bullets; *non-LLM code* appends new bullets with fresh IDs or bumps existing counters in place, with embedding-based dedup and a grow-and-refine consolidation pass triggered proactively or lazily on context-budget overflow. The full-rewrite ablation (66.7%→57.1%) is the strongest single argument in the corpus for this split: LLM proposes, deterministic code disposes.

**Insert-time neighbor evolution.** A-MEM: when a new note arrives, its top-k embedding neighbors are shown to an LLM that decides links *and* may rewrite the neighbors' retrieval-facing metadata (should_evolve, strengthen/update_neighbor, new tags/context) — the most explicit "new evidence updates old knowledge" mechanism found, worth ~+5.7 multi-hop and ~+14.6 temporal F1 in ablation on top of link generation. Its weakness is equally instructive: rewrites are unvalidated, in-place, and rollback-free — a bad evolution silently corrupts established notes. (Original content stays immutable; only metadata evolves — a split kapso should keep and harden with versioning.)

**Constrained edit-in-place (the preserve ledger).** SkillRevise: when the learner edits an existing unit rather than appending a new one, the edit must carry explicit preservation constraints protecting the measured behavior the old unit already got right — and this discipline is *validated*: removing the preserve ledger costs −12.8pp in ablation, making it the corpus's only tested edit-in-place protocol. The card maps it directly onto kapso: a card's TESTED-KEPT evidence entries are the constraint set — an edit to an existing bank card may extend or re-scope it but must not contradict behavior those entries measured, turning card edits into constrained revision rather than blind rewrite.

**Key-matched appending.** AutoGuide: a new context string is LLM-matched against existing context keys; match → append the guideline under it, no match → new entry. Documented failure: paraphrase fragmentation ("contexts that describe the same situation but expressed slightly differently") splits the dictionary — an argument for a periodic consolidation pass over keys, which AutoGuide lacks.

**Capacity-triggered consolidation.** AutoManual's Consolidator fires only when the rule count exceeds its cap (12), inspects supporting trajectories via each rule's Validation Logs before merging or deleting — consolidation reads evidence, not just text. ACE's lazy refinement is the same trigger pattern without the evidence-reading.

**Merge-by-construction.** RoMeRL replaces merging with bounded slots and dominance rules (at most one representative per coordinate; a new trajectory must beat the incumbent under a role-specific rule, inheriting its utility as warm start); DreamCoder gets dedup for free from its objective (once a fragment is admitted, a near-duplicate has ~zero marginal compression and auto-loses, or routes naturally to "this is an edit of the existing primitive"); the survey catalogs clustering-similar-trajectories-then-compressing-jointly as merge-before-the-fact.

**Whole-memory regeneration (the anti-pattern).** CLIN (rewrite everything each trial, dedup delegated to one prompt) and Dynamic Cheatsheet (holistic rewrite) — both destroy identity, provenance, and history, and DC documents the degradation mechanism directly.

### 6.2 Conflict resolution: the honest answer

**Almost nobody solves contradiction between stored units, and the cards support saying so explicitly.** The closest approximations: ExpeL's DOWNVOTE-to-zero-then-remove (soft, opinion-driven, extraction-time only); AutoManual's Builder rewriting a rule when an Imperfect-Rules episode exposes it (reactive, requires the rule to visibly cause a failure); TF-GRPO's delete/modify ops (LLM-noticed, no accumulating contradiction evidence); MemRL and Memento's stance that contradictory items coexist and the higher-scored one wins retrieval (arbitration, not resolution). ACE explicitly has no conflict story for contradictory bullets; A-MEM lets contradictory notes coexist and both retrieve; AWM contributes the sharpest *negative* merge result — naively unioning offline-induced and online-induced workflow sets performs worse than either alone (Table 11), evidence that provenance-blind merging actively hurts and that cards from different sources need provenance tags and a cross-provenance conflict check before pooling.

For kapso this means contradiction handling is largely greenfield. What the literature does support: detect candidate conflicts at merge time (embedding neighbors + an explicit LLM contradiction check per neighbor, A-MEM-style), and resolve by *evidence*, not text — kapso cards cite measured deltas, so two contradictory Heuristics can often be reconciled into one scope-conditioned card ("X helps on task family A [delta, run], hurts on B [delta, run]"), which is AutoGuide's context-conditioning applied as a conflict-resolution move. No paper in the corpus does this; the pieces exist, the assembly is novel.

One decision the table implies but the pipeline must make explicit: **the bank of cards is not the only retrieval substrate — adopt ExpeL's two channels.** Its ablation shows abstract insights and verbatim exemplars are complementary, not redundant (insights-only 36 / retrieval-only 31 / both 39 on HotpotQA), and the ExpeL card maps the exemplar channel directly onto kapso: archived runs (code snapshot + metrics) retrieved by task similarity play the trajectory-exemplar role, injected as raw exemplars into ideation/implementation alongside the cards. Memento is the scoring/retrieval layer for exactly this second channel — its card's own instruction is to compose it with an abstraction layer, not to replace one. So the runs archive is double-duty: verification substrate for code cards (§7.3(4)) *and* a similarity-retrieved episodic exemplar store (small k, per §5.3(4) ranking) — not an either/or with the cards.

### 7.2 Minimal card schema the literature supports

Every card should carry: **(1) typed content** (kapso's existing Principle/Heuristic/Implementation/Environment/Workflow types map onto AutoManual's six-type taxonomy, which contributes one missing type worth adopting: an "Unsolved Error" open-problem card; AutoManual's remove-the-type ablation, 97.4→91.5, says typing pays); **(2) scope-first applicability conditions** (AutoGuide context key + AutoManual's mandatory scope-first sentence; machine-checkable preconditions for code cards per Mobile-Agent-E); **(3) evidence citations** (Generative Agents' "because of 1, 2, 8, 15" pointing at specific features_history lines, technical_difficulties entries, archived runs — making cards auditable and re-checkable); **(4) reliability state** — score, confirm/contradict counts, visit counter, last-exercised timestamp (MemRL Q + RoMeRL cold-tracking + ACE counters), plus a per-context **unverified-in-this-context flag**: a meta-card entering a task family where it has never been exercised keeps the flag until a campaign experiment confirms it there (CLIN's over-generalization-at-first-use warning, §3.2 — a lifecycle state distinct from cold, since the card may be well-tested elsewhere), with the confidence also printed into the card text so the reading LLM weighs it (CLIN); **(5) provenance tags** — source campaign, task family, seeded-vs-learned (AWM's union-hurts result), and the authoring model; **(6) a grounding example** (AutoManual; Voyager's description-as-key: for code cards, embed the LLM-written description, not the code).

**Cross-model validity.** Kapso's model config is user-owned and can change between campaigns, so the schema must take a position on whether cards outlive the backbone that wrote them. Three cards bear on it, and they agree on a split verdict: knowledge mostly transfers — AutoManual's GPT-4-built manual boosts a GPT-3.5 planner, and RoMeRL's frozen memory state keeps +1.4 to +14.6 gains across backbones (the learned utilities encode task knowledge, not backbone quirks) — but *procedural* units degrade, because SkillRevise finds revised skills lose 8–15pp under a different executor than the one they were revised against: they entangle reusable procedure with executor-specific convention. Design rule: treat text cards as approximately model-agnostic; for code/workflow cards, strip executor-specific convention at lint time (§7.3(3), exactly as SkillRevise's absorption filters strip verifier-specific shortcuts), tag every card with its authoring model (item 5), and expect — and attribute via the tag — score degradation after a model swap rather than blaming the card's content.

### 7.3 The pipeline the literature best supports

Stages, each with its precedent; details the evidence does not decide are flagged.

**When the learner runs.** The corpus splits cleanly on timing, and the review must take a side: online per-trajectory updating (AutoManual — whose 97.4→77.6 offline-extraction ablation is the largest single-factor result in the corpus; AWM, whose online induction recovers the cross-website/domain generalization offline induction loses, 33.9/35.5 vs 18.6; ASI's test-time induction; CLIN's per-trial regeneration) versus offline batch extraction (ExpeL, AutoGuide, Learn-by-interact, DS-Agent's seed bank). The online side holds the two strongest results, and both for the same reason: units get validated by the practice that follows them. The rule this supports for kapso: run stages 1–5 **incrementally within the campaign** — between evolve iterations, at natural batch points — so freshly minted cards are exercised (and scored) by the campaign's own subsequent experiments, AutoManual-style; run a **post-campaign consolidation pass** (ExpeL's L-batches, the survey's cluster-then-compress) for cross-campaign meta-cards. Whether mid-campaign passes are worth their latency and spend at kapso's episode cost is a genuinely open trade the toy-scale evidence cannot settle — but the default should be incremental-plus-consolidation, not learn-once-at-the-end.

1. **Triage (gate).** Classify each candidate/campaign outcome — direct success, indirect success (recovered failure), failure — and attribute cause: Imperfect Knowledge vs Imperfect Agent (AutoManual's case-conditioned prompting, credited with reducing hallucinated rules). Only Imperfect-Knowledge branches touch the bank. With kapso's noisy scalar deltas, add a significance threshold before treating a delta as a win/loss (TF-GRPO's binary winner/loser split needs a score-gap threshold in continuous-score regimes) — *the threshold itself is an open tuning choice*.
2. **Extraction (Reflector).** Contrastive mining of within-iteration lane groups and KEPT/REJECTED pairs (TF-GRPO/ExpeL/AutoGuide), backward-construction relabeling of trajectory segments (Learn-by-interact), at fine granularity (one insight per card, ACE; decision-scale, not campaign-scale). Emit 2–3 generality variants per candidate card and select by an explicit scorer rather than trusting one LLM guess (DreamCoder's granularity-by-objective, adapted) — with the scorer named per representation: for code-card variants it is deterministic (marginal compression over past winning solutions, DreamCoder's admission objective); for text-card variants the only available analogue is an LLM-judged marginal-information test against past evidence, which the DreamCoder card itself rates "weaker but still usable" — so text-variant selection is *not* deterministic and stays an open choice. Separate prompts for text-card vs code-card mining (Mobile-Agent-E's two reflectors). Iterative refinement of the reflection is worth points (ACE: −3.4 TGC without it). And run extraction/curation on the strongest model available: five cards converge on quality collapsing with a weaker learner — ExpeL's extraction drops 39%→32% with a gpt-3.5 extractor, Voyager's GPT-3.5 coder yields 5.7× fewer items, Dynamic Cheatsheet's gains shrink to minimal or negative on GPT-4o-mini/Haiku (and R1's verbosity made its memory unreliable), TF-GRPO actively *hurts* QwQ-32B (25.5% on the web task, below its own ReAct baseline), and AutoManual concedes weaker Builders build worse manuals — a weak-model learning pass is worse than no pass.
3. **Lint + committee (filter).** Apply SkillRevise's defect taxonomy as card lint (reject context_pollution, false_certainty, over_specificity — strip dataset-specific literals, paths, grader shortcuts exactly as SkillRevise's absorption filters do), then an all-must-agree LLM committee for coherence (Learn-by-interact) — cheap, but explicitly a plausibility check, not validation.
4. **Verification (admission).** Text cards: admitted only with cited grader-backed evidence. Code cards: ASI's replay gate against the runs archive (correctness vs recorded grader score + actually-invoked + observable effect); expect a low pass rate (~15.6% is ASI's prior), which with kapso's few candidates per campaign means near-zero code cards per campaign — the deliberate arithmetic of §5.3's split bets: text cards carry most of the bank's growth, and code-card scarcity is the price of the hard gate, not a bug. *Open: how much replay compute per learning pass is worth spending — replay is far more expensive in kapso's regime than in ASI's 5-step episodes.*
5. **Merge (Curator).** LLM proposes typed operations with the relevant bank slice in context (ExpeL/TF-GRPO/AutoManual); deterministic code applies them (ACE) — append-with-new-id, counter/score updates in place, embedding dedup. Per new card, run A-MEM-style neighbor evolution (versioned metadata edits, immutable card bodies) plus an explicit contradiction check, resolving conflicts by scope-conditioning on cited evidence where possible (novel — see §6.2). Consolidation runs as a separate evidence-reading pass on a capacity/overlap trigger (AutoManual), never folded into the write path. **Concurrency**: Dynamic Cheatsheet's card flags the failure mode directly — a read-modify-write memory loop resists parallel execution, and kapso runs K parallel lanes per iteration (and potentially concurrent campaigns) against one bank. The delta design is also the concurrency answer: lanes never write to the bank; each emits delta proposals into a per-lane queue, and the single-writer deterministic Curator merges the queues at the iteration boundary (or batch-at-harvest for cross-campaign passes) — ACE's LLM-proposes/code-disposes split makes the deltas serializable where DC's whole-blob rewrite cannot be.
6. **Lifecycle (continuous).** The §5.3 composite: attribution-gated EMA score updates from each subsequent campaign's graded outcomes, visit counters, confirmation-driven decay, ranked retrieval (similarity + reliability, small k: 2–4 per AutoGuide/Memento/DS-Agent), re-retrieval at lens pivots (companion study's Cond-Step), archive-not-delete retirement. Plus AutoManual's fallback routing for cold task families: when kapso has no proven recipe for a task family (no skill/Workflow card exists), serve the recorded failure knowledge instead — inject the family's technical_difficulties-derived anti-pattern cards in place of the missing recipe (AutoManual's "no skill for this task type → serve the reflection" rule) — a cheap retrieval rule that gives new task families negative guidance before they have positive precedent.

## 8. Open Problems — Where Kapso Will Be Doing Novel Work

1. **Scoring under extreme signal scarcity.** MemRL's Q converges over hundreds of cheap binary episodes; RoMeRL's Theorem 2 says estimating N utilities needs O(N log N/ε²) feedback events. Kapso gets perhaps tens of graded outcomes per campaign and few campaigns per card lifetime. This *strengthens* RoMeRL's case for few, role-bounded, attributably-scored cards over a large uniformly-scored bank, and forces large α, mandatory visit counters, and honest "cold" states — but no paper has validated a scored lifecycle in this regime. The endorsement must also carry RoMeRL's own costs, which its card is explicit about: the 2×2 slot taxonomy is hand-designed, not learned; four slots is aggressive forgetting (a rare-but-valuable third success mode is simply discarded, and the ablations show slot *contents* matter — dropping the proven-informative-failure slot hurts final and cumulative SR, dropping the recovery slot hurts current performance); and the whole scheme "assumes a meaningful task-grouping exists." That last assumption exposes a kapso-mapping gap this review has not closed: nothing yet defines how task families/groups are drawn across RelBench task types, and both the bounded-slot proposal and per-family scoring presuppose that definition. The bank-size-vs-expected-feedback budget is a design decision the literature only bounds, not solves.
2. **Credit assignment across a multi-hour campaign.** RoMeRL proves outcome-level reward cannot remove attribution bias and names counterfactual paired rollouts as the true fix — unaffordable at kapso's episode cost. Judge-verdict-based attribution (which cited cards were load-bearing) is a cheap proxy with no measured error rate anywhere in the corpus; auditing it (e.g., via RoMeRL's inject-known-noise MRT protocol adapted to cards) is novel work.
3. **Continuous, noisy rewards.** Nearly every scoring mechanism reviewed assumes binary success (Memento notes the coarseness explicitly; DreamCoder's authors name pervasive noise as their open challenge; AutoManual's five-case triage presumes crisp outcomes). Converting noisy scalar validation deltas into confirm/contradict events — thresholds, replication requirements, variance handling — is undecided by the literature.
4. **Contradiction between cards.** As §6.2 establishes, no system resolves it; arbitration-by-score and opinion-driven downvotes are the state of the art. Evidence-grounded reconciliation into scope-conditioned cards is unexplored.
5. **Merging quantitative evidence.** Kapso cards carry measured deltas; no paper merges *numbers* (e.g., two campaigns measuring the same feature's effect at different magnitudes). Whether to pool, range, or condition on task family is greenfield.
6. **Heterogeneous task distributions.** MemRL plateaus exactly on its most heterogeneous benchmark (HLE, intra-task similarity 0.186), Learn-by-interact concedes weak cross-domain transfer, and DS-Agent — the closest-domain system — documents the coverage failure directly: novel modalities (e.g. graph data) fail until the human bank is manually expanded, a warning aimed squarely at kapso's relational/graph RelBench regime, where the seeded bank's modality coverage is a live risk, not a hypothetical. Kapso spans RelBench task types. The literature's partial answers — task-family scoping (RoMeRL's task groups, AWM's per-website memories), two-tier instance/meta cards (CLIN), state-conditional utility (Memento) — have not been combined or tested at kapso's heterogeneity.
7. **Cards exercised implicitly.** A card can shape ideation without being cited (an idea "in the air" of the injected context). ACE's flag-what-you-used attribution assumes honest, complete self-reporting; nothing measures silent influence, and unattributed exercise means missed score updates in a regime that can't afford to miss any.
8. **Memory reward-hacking.** No paper defends against an agent gaming its memory's scores (ACE states this gap). Kapso's separation of ideation/implementation agents from the immutable grader and a deterministic curator narrows the surface, but the adversarial analysis does not exist in the literature.
9. **Evaluating the learner itself.** The survey states experience benchmarks are "significantly insufficient." Reusable pieces: RoMeRL's MRT stress test, AWM's utility rate, the companion study's FWT/BWT framing, MemRL's Q-vs-success-bin calibration — but a benchmark for trajectory-learning in expensive-experiment ML engineering does not exist.

## 9. Annotated Bibliography

**Reflexion (Shinn et al., arXiv 2303.11366, NeurIPS 2023).** The foundational signal-to-text mechanism: a Self-Reflection LLM converts sparse outcome signals into short verbal self-critiques stored in an episodic buffer (Ω=1–3, task-local, FIFO) and prepended to the next retry of the same task. AlfWorld 130/134 vs ReAct's ~108/134; HumanEval Python 91.0% vs GPT-4's 80.1%. Two results matter most for kapso: the +8% ablation gap between abstracted reflections and raw-trajectory memory (80% vs 72% HotPotQA), justifying an abstraction stage; and the MBPP loss (77.1 vs 80.1) caused by 16.3%-false-positive self-generated tests — the canonical corrupted-evaluator warning that reliability updates must come only from trustworthy graders. No cross-task bank, no scoring, no merging; the "level 0" baseline kapso's implementation agents already implicitly reproduce via technical_difficulties.

**ExpeL (Zhao et al., arXiv 2308.10144, AAAI 2024).** Lifts Reflexion's reflections into a cross-task insight pool maintained by ADD/UPVOTE/DOWNVOTE/EDIT operations with an importance counter (ADD=2, remove at 0), extracted by a stronger LLM from contrastive fail/success pairs and success batches; plus a complementary channel of retrieved raw success trajectories. HotpotQA 39% vs ReAct 28%; the two memory channels are complementary (36/31 alone → 39 together); learned insights beat hand-crafted human ones (39% vs 32%); HotpotQA-learned insights transfer to FEVER (70 vs 63). Its central gap defines Q3: counts freeze after extraction and encode extractor opinion, never downstream outcomes — the missing feedback loop kapso's reliability score supplies. The operation vocabulary is the cleanest edit-vs-add API in the corpus.

**AutoGuide (Fu et al., arXiv 2403.08978, NeurIPS 2024).** Splits learned knowledge into WHEN (an LLM-abstracted context key from the shared trajectory prefix) and WHAT (a guideline from the success/failure divergence), stored as a dictionary keyed by context and retrieved by abstracting the agent's current state. WebArena-Reddit 47.1% vs ExpeL 21.8% from only 19 demonstrations; the WebShop decomposition (30→36→37→46) isolates state-conditioning as the driver; k=5 injected guidelines *hurts* vs k=2–3. No lifecycle whatsoever (admitted: "no quantifiable metric of guideline quality"), context-key dedup fragments under paraphrase, and n=1-evidence guidelines are trusted forever — the applicability-conditioning donor and a lifecycle cautionary tale.

**AutoManual (Chen et al., arXiv 2405.16247, NeurIPS 2024).** Online Planner/Builder alternation: the Builder classifies each trajectory into five cases (direct/indirect success, failure; imperfect-rules vs imperfect-agent) and applies case-specific rule management via write_rule/update_rule tools over a ≤12-rule set with four attributes (type from six categories, scope-first content, grounding example, validation logs); a Consolidator merges on overflow; a Formulator compiles a transferable manual. ALFWorld 97.4% (GPT-4-turbo) vs Reflexion 85.9%/ExpeL 79.2% from one human example; the offline-extraction ablation (97.4→77.6) is the corpus's strongest evidence that validating rules by practice is load-bearing. Contributes kapso's triage gate (imperfect-agent branches write nothing), scope-first phrasing, the "Unsolved Error" card type, and evidence-reading consolidation — but no numeric score.

**CLIN (Majumder et al., arXiv 2310.10134).** Persistent textual memory of causal abstractions in four rigid templates ("X SHOULD/MAY BE NECESSARY to Y", "X DOES NOT/MAY CONTRIBUTE to Y") with confidence encoded linguistically; whole memory regenerated per trial; meta-memory distilled from best-trial memories (archive 10) for environment/task transfer. ScienceWorld: 62.2 vs Reflexion 39.4 on adaptation; structured causal memory beats free-form advice by 6.2 points — direct evidence for typed cards over prose. Pathologies: wrong insights repeatedly retrieved with no demotion (stove/oven), transferred insights over-generalizing in new settings, and full-rewrite merging destroying provenance — three specific anti-patterns kapso's design must avoid. Its causal templates map almost mechanically onto features_history entries.

**Generative Agents (Park et al., arXiv 2304.03442, UIST 2023) + MemoryBank (Zhong et al., arXiv 2305.10250, AAAI 2024).** The memory stream with the canonical retrieval formula (recency with 0.995/hr access-refreshed decay + one-shot LLM importance 1–10 + embedding relevance, equal weights, min-max normalized) and importance-gated reflection producing *cited* insight trees (leaves = observations, upper nodes = abstractions). Ablation: full architecture TrueSkill μ=29.89 vs no-reflection 26.88 vs no-memory 21.21. MemoryBank adds Ebbinghaus retention R=e^(−t/S), S+1 per recall. Both reinforce on retrieval frequency — a popularity loop kapso must replace with outcome-driven updates (increment S on *confirmation*) — but the citation-tree provenance and the batch-reflection trigger transfer directly. Evaluation is believability, not task success; toy-scale caveat applies strongly.

**ACE / Agentic Context Engineering (Zhang et al., arXiv 2510.04618, ICLR 2026).** Evolving "playbook" of itemized bullets with IDs and helpful/harmful counters, grown by Generator (flags used bullets) → Reflector (extracts lessons) → Curator (emits delta bullets merged by *deterministic non-LLM logic* with embedding dedup and lazy grow-and-refine). Motivated by context collapse: monolithic LLM rewrite compressed 18,282→122 tokens, accuracy 66.7%→57.1%. +10.6% avg on agent benchmarks; works from execution feedback alone (+14.8% without labels); quality degrades with weak feedback. The Q4 donor (LLM proposes, code disposes) and the closest precedent for use-time attribution counters — though retirement is never counter-driven and counter-gaming is undefended.

**Dynamic Cheatsheet (Suzgun et al., arXiv 2504.07952).** A single self-curated text memory rewritten by a curator LLM per query, holding strategies *and* inline code. Game of 24: GPT-4o 10%→99% via a stored Python solver — the corpus's strongest single datum for code-card value; Claude AIME 2024 23.3%→50.0% while uncurated full history got 26.7%, and GPT-4o full-history fell *below* baseline (13.3% vs 20%) — raw accumulation actively harms. No validation, no scores; documented rewrite degradation (models abbreviate instead of re-emitting) and error propagation through embedding-similar queries. Its lacks are kapso's justifications: discrete card identity, outcome-gated curation, reliability scoring.

**Training-Free GRPO (Cai et al., arXiv 2510.08191).** Reproduces GRPO's group-relative structure in token space: G rollouts per query conditioned on an experiential library; only mixed-outcome groups yield a "semantic advantage" (NL contrast of winners vs losers), applied to the library via add/delete/modify/keep/merge with the library in context. AIME24 80.0→82.7, AIME25 67.9→73.3 from 100 samples/~$18; group-size-1 ablation loses most of the gain; one-shot reflection without the loop fails entirely. Maps beautifully onto kapso's K parallel lanes as natural groups, with the caveat that uniform-outcome iterations teach nothing and continuous scores need gap thresholds. No per-unit scores; 3-step runs leave long-horizon library health untested.

**Memento (Zhou et al., arXiv 2508.16153).** "Don't abstract, select": raw (task, plan, binary reward) cases, successes and failures alike, with a case-selection policy trained online — Q(s, case) via BCE on outcomes, kernel episodic control — retrieving K=4 mixed exemplars into the planner. GAIA 87.88% Pass@3; memory adds +4.7–9.6 points OOD; parametric > non-parametric > no memory over 5 iterations; K>8 hurts. Its own deflating caveats, which calibrate expectations for a scoring layer alone: in the Table 5 decomposition, memory's marginal contribution (+3.7–8.2 F1/PM) is small next to planning's (+11–32.5 F1); benefits saturate after 3–5 iterations (~33k cases); and GAIA Pass@3 flatters the headline. Contributes the state-*conditional* reliability idea (a card is good *for* similar contexts, not globally), first-class failure storage, and the small-K injection warning. No abstraction, no merge — explicitly the scoring/retrieval layer to compose with an abstraction layer.

**MemRL (Zhang et al., arXiv 2601.03192).** RL over episodic memory with a frozen LLM: (intent embedding, experience summary, Q) triplets; two-phase retrieval (similarity recall, then rank by (1−λ)·sim + λ·Q, both z-score normalized, λ=0.5); Monte-Carlo update Q ← Q + α(r−Q) for injected memories only. Q genuinely predicts success (21.5%→88.1% across bins, r=0.861); ~12% of failure-derived memories earn top-bin Q; normalization removal raises forgetting 0.041→0.073. The reliability-update rule kapso can adopt near-verbatim — with attribution gating added (its uniform credit across injected memories is its acknowledged flaw) and a visit counter (its Q converges over hundreds of episodes kapso won't have). Plateaus on heterogeneous HLE; no merge, no pruning, unbounded growth.

**RoMeRL (Yang et al., arXiv 2608.02508).** The failure-mode paper for scored memory. Formalizes the memory-reward trap (co-retrieved non-contributing items accumulate positive utility under episode-level reward; attribution bias irreducible by more feedback — Theorem 1) and feedback dilution (O(N log N/ε²) requirement; ~45% cold-Q items in practice). Fix: a reduced-order 2×2 state per task group (PCC/PAC/NCC/NAC: best success, recovery exemplar, proven-useful failure, freshest failure), replacement with utility inheritance instead of unbounded scored items — cold-Q 44.9%→9.0%, ~6× feedback density, noise items get 2.4 vs 7.2 misattributed update mass vs MemRL+UCB. Directly shapes kapso's attribution-gated scoring, cold-state tracking, bounded per-task-family slots, and the reusable MRT stress-test protocol. Regime caveat: works because of hundreds of repeated episodes per group — which *strengthens* its argument in kapso's scarcer regime.

**DS-Agent (Guo et al., arXiv 2402.17453, ICML 2024).** Case-based reasoning for automated data science — the closest domain to kapso. Dual banks: human Kaggle-winner insight cases (text, via a fixed six-question code→insight prompt) and agent (task, code) cases retained only on measured score improvement. Development: 100% build success, avg best rank 1.5 vs baseline 5.4; deployment one-pass ~99% (GPT-4) at ~$0.13/task; homogeneous task+code cases beat textual insights for one-pass code generation; k=1 case in context beats more (interference). Contributes the text→ideation / code→implementation routing, the admission-gate template, the six-question extraction schema, and ReviseRank (re-rank retrieved cases against current execution feedback). Success-only retention and zero post-admission lifecycle are its gaps, plus two caveats its card flags: bank coverage is a hard dependence — novel modalities (e.g. graph data) fail until the human bank is manually expanded (§8(6)) — and the retain step's marginal value (agent bank vs seed-only bank) is only indirectly ablated.

**Learn-by-interact (Su et al., arXiv 2501.10893, ICLR 2025).** Label-free bank synthesis: self-instructed tasks, rollouts, then *backward construction* — relabel every nested sub-trajectory with a fresh instruction describing what it actually did, salvaging failed/drifted rollouts — filtered by an all-must-agree LLM committee (25–50% pass), used as SFT data or state-keyed ICL exemplars. WebArena 48.0 vs 35.8 (Claude ICL); removing backward construction collapses gains to ~2%; short (<5-step) units most effective; state-keyed agentic retrieval beats instruction-keyed RAG (~3–4%). For kapso: relabel trajectory segments by measured effect (with the grader supplying the outcome verification this paper lacks), multi-granularity extraction, committee-as-pre-filter, and mid-campaign re-retrieval keyed on current state.

**Voyager (Wang et al., arXiv 2305.16291, TMLR 2024).** The canonical verify-before-store code-skill library: GPT-4-written JavaScript skills, iteratively refined against environment feedback and execution errors (≤4 rounds), admitted only after a self-verification critic confirms environment-attested success; indexed by embeddings of LLM-written descriptions; top-5 retrieved for compositional reuse. 3.3× item discovery, only method to reach diamond tools (in 1 of 3 trials), 4/4 held-out tasks in a fresh world vs 0/4 for all baselines; ablations: no-verification ≈ −73% discoveries (the most valuable component), no-library ≈ −73% and plateau. The library is also plug-in reusable by other agents (it boosts AutoGPT when given access) — mild but real precedent for one bank serving kapso's different agent roles. Append-only forever — no scores, no retirement — with documented stuck-agent and hallucination failures; the admission-gate donor whose missing lifecycle is kapso's Q3 mandate.

**AWM / Agent Workflow Memory (Wang et al., arXiv 2409.07429).** Induces variable-abstracted *sub-task* workflows from successful trajectories (rule-based or LM), injected wholesale (~7/site) into the prompt; online variant admits via an LLM success judge in streaming fashion. WebArena 35.5% vs 23.5%; online induction recovers cross-website/domain generalization (33.9/35.5 vs 18.6); text ≈ code for injected guidance (45.4 vs 45.1); callable-macro variant largely fails (+1.3, 18.5% invocation). Documents two negative results kapso must design around: judge false-positives permanently contaminate memory (no rescoring exists), and offline+online workflow *union hurts* — the provenance-aware-merging warrant. Utility rate (0.91–0.94) is the usage metric to make live.

**ASI / Agent Skill Induction (Wang et al., arXiv 2504.06821).** Online programmatic-skill induction with an execution-based admission gate: rewrite the source trajectory to call the candidate skill, replay, require correctness + actual usage + observable effect (~15.6% pass); verified skills join the *action space*. 40.4% vs AWM 36.3% vs static 32.7%, with fewer steps; the ablation ladder (unverified 32.6 < verified-in-memory 36.4 < verified-text 39.0 < verified-as-actions 40.1) separates verification from injection-site effects. Episode cleaning before induction (87.9→13.4 tokens/step). No post-admission lifecycle (deprecated skills linger), no dedup; kapso's grader replaces its LM-judge weakness, and its replay gate is the code-card admission template.

**SkillRevise (Liu et al., arXiv 2606.01139).** Demonstrates one-shot LLM-authored skills are behaviorally weak (39.53% vs 36.05% no-skill) and repairable by a bounded execute→diagnose→revise loop (61.63% at 3 rounds), guided by a structured Diagnosis (verifier spec + failure attribution + preservation constraints; −29pp without it) and a small retrieved Principle Memory of repair patterns. Contributes: the never-bank-unexercised-cards rule; the Diagnosis triple mapping onto grader contract/technical_difficulties/TESTED-KEPT ledger; the defect taxonomy as card lint (context_pollution 207/208, false_certainty 181/208, over_specificity and wrong_abstraction_level 54/208 each); the multi-term utility U (success, efficiency, transfer, −interference); and the no-skill-fallback lesson. No decay/retirement; frozen principle memory in evaluation.

**DreamCoder (Ellis et al., arXiv 2006.08381, PLDI 2021).** Wake-sleep Bayesian program learning: sleep-abstraction refactors the corpus of verified solutions (version-space/e-graph machinery over ~10^14 candidate rewritings) and admits into the library exactly the fragment maximizing corpus compression (MDL), at the parameterization the objective selects; a recognition network soft-retrieves primitives per task. Text editing 79.6%/108 in 10 min; library depth correlates with solve rate r=0.79. The principled answer to "when does a code abstraction deserve to exist" — non-gameable, dedup-for-free, granularity-by-objective — but add-only, success-only, noise-intolerant, and statistically hungry; kapso should borrow the admission *criterion* (via Stitch-like tooling) and the prior-weighted soft-retirement pattern, not the machinery.

**Mobile-Agent-E (Wang et al., arXiv 2501.11733).** Hierarchical mobile-GUI agent whose self-evolution module is the cleanest structural precedent for kapso's card split: two dedicated Experience Reflectors distill each trajectory (+ error history + existing memory) into Tips (text) and Shortcuts (executable JSON macros with explicit precondition fields), injected at different sites (Shortcuts → planner + action space; Tips → operator). Evolution alone adds ~11.8 Satisfaction points (75.1→86.9) and cuts termination errors 32%→12% on Mobile-Eval-E (25 tasks — toy scale, human-rubric scored) — but with no ablation separating Tips-only from Shortcuts-only in the main table, the +11.8 supports evolution as a whole, not the two-representation design specifically; where this review invokes the split (§4.1, §7.3(2)) it borrows the *structure*, with the representation-routing evidence coming from DC/AWM/ASI (§4.2). Its two documented failures — precondition misuse and erroneous self-generated shortcuts persisting unrevised — are the strongest argument-by-negative-example for machine-checkable preconditions plus execution validation plus a reliability lifecycle, all three of which the authors name as future work.

**A-MEM (Xu et al., arXiv 2502.12110, NeurIPS 2025).** Zettelkasten-style agentic memory: each interaction becomes a note (immutable content + LLM keywords/tags/one-sentence context + embedding + links); on insert, top-k neighbors are shown to an LLM that creates links *and* may rewrite neighbors' metadata ("memory evolution") — the most explicit insert-time consolidation mechanism found, worth ~+11.7 multi-hop F1 (links) plus ~+5.7 (evolution) in ablation; temporal QA 45.85 vs MemGPT 25.52; 85–93% token reduction. No scores, no decay, no conflict handling, no rollback on evolution — the merge-mechanism donor (hardened with versioning), emphatically not the lifecycle donor; several deltas (multi-hop +0.37) are within noise, and the setting is conversational QA, not task execution.

**Survey: "From Storage to Experience" (Luo et al., arXiv 2605.06716, ACL 2026 Findings) + companion study (Hu, Long, Wang, arXiv 2604.27003).** The organizing frame: Storage → Reflection (refined but context-bound) → Experience (cross-trajectory, scenario-detached, MDL-constrained |K| ≪ Σ|τ|), with explicit/implicit/hybrid experience routes and catalogs of validation, decay, and consolidation families. The companion study supplies the corpus's most controlled representation evidence: insights vs raw trajectories give ALFWorld forward transfer +6.5% vs −9.5% (raw's damage concentrated on hard cases, ΔNL −26.1); fine-grained entries maximize forward transfer (+15.0 vs +9.0) at the worst forgetting (BWT −10.0); mid-episode re-querying flips B→A transfer from −1.0 to +7.5; an unmanaged pool yields 88.5% identical-retrieval collapse. Together: the Reflection/Experience boundary as kapso's abstraction principle, and the unmanaged-pool pathologies as the reliability-score motivation.
